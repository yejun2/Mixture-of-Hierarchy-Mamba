R5-Q1: Limitation

R5-A1: In some cases, low-texture background regions (e.g., roads) can impair fine-grained recognition, as models tend to focus on highly textured areas (e.g., lane markings) while ignoring visually uniform ones. To mitigate this issue, recent methods encourage models to explicitly identify and treat uniform, low-texture regions as background. Incorporating such strategies can help address the limitations caused by low-texture areas and enhance overall recognition performance.

R5-Q4: ReSO Impact

R5-A4: The ReSO strategy has minimal impact on training time. Small object identification runs **only once during training (at epoch 10)**, taking about **35 ms per image** (assuming two detected objects). The rest of the pipeline follows standard procedures, but the number of training samples generated by ReSO can increase in the following two cases:

1.  **Larger $\epsilon$** expands the spatial range considered as object regions.

2.  **Higher $\gamma$** shrinks the masking region, so smaller areas are treated as candidate objects.

As shown in Tab. 1, increasing the number of samples (time) does not necessarily lead to better performance. Higher $\gamma$ may mistake object parts for full objects, and larger $\epsilon$ can blur the distinction of truly small instances. A proper range of hyperparameters was found to be effective for optimal small object identification, which we adopted in our approach.
